## My AI Journey So Far

I’ve always been in tech.

I studied computer science, but pretty early on I realized I didn’t want to be a full-time developer. What I enjoyed more was being closer to the problem space: working with engineers, shaping ideas, and actually shipping products. That’s how I ended up in product management.

Even then, I was never far from the technical side. I’ve written SQL, worked with production systems, and spent years collaborating closely with developers. I understood how software gets built.

So when AI started getting attention, I wasn’t coming in as a beginner—but I also wasn’t convinced.

---

At first, it felt overhyped.

Mostly like smarter autocomplete. Predicting words, generating text, but not really understanding anything. I didn’t see how it would meaningfully change how I work.

But curiosity got the better of me, and I started digging deeper. Not just trying tools, but actually learning: reading books, going through the basics, and even forcing myself through some of the math behind it.

At some point, it stopped being hype.

---

The real shift didn’t come from theory, though. It came from using it in my day-to-day work.

Small things at first: writing SQL queries faster, drafting product requirement documents, exploring ideas when I was stuck. Nothing revolutionary on its own, but consistently useful.

Then came the moment that really changed things for me: when AI started being connected to real-time information.

Suddenly, it wasn’t limited to old training data anymore. It became something I could actually rely on in real scenarios. And more importantly, it started saving me time.

That’s when I made a decision: I’m going to use this every day and see how far it goes.

---

Over time, my way of working started to shift.

Not overnight, but gradually. I became faster. Tasks that used to take hours started taking minutes. I started using AI as a thinking partner: something to challenge ideas, suggest directions, or help me get unstuck.

But one thing I realized pretty quickly: AI is powerful, but without structure, it’s easy to build the wrong thing faster.

---

Diving deeper into topics like AI agents, RAG systems, and how language models are built made it clear to me that this isn’t just another tool. It’s a shift in how software, and maybe even products, will be built.

---

### Books that shaped my understanding

Along the way, I went pretty deep into learning. These are some of the books that helped me most:

- *[Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)](https://www.amazon.de/dp/1633437167)* — Sebastian Raschka  
- *[Why Machines Learn](https://www.amazon.de/dp/0593185749)* — Anil Ananthaswamy  
- *[AI Literacy Fundamentals](https://www.amazon.de/dp/1960907077)* — Ben Jones  
- *[Agentic Artificial Intelligence: Harnessing AI Agents to Reinvent Business, Work and Life](https://www.amazon.de/dp/B0F1KKYX4T)* — Pascal Bornet  
- *[Making Sense of Generative AI: Cutting through the hype for business leaders and curious minds](https://www.amazon.de/dp/3982701902)* — Dr. Dominik Hörndlein  
- *[Architecting AI Software Systems: Crafting robust and scalable AI systems for modern software development](https://www.amazon.de/dp/1804615978)* — Richard D. Avila, Imran Ahmad  
- *[Unlocking Data with Generative AI and RAG](https://amzn.eu/d/04JQCVMB)* — Keith Bourne  

---

Today, AI is part of almost everything I do.

I use it for writing SQL and code, for brainstorming, for summarizing, and for learning new things faster. I’ve even experimented with things like generating videos, just to see how far things can go.

It’s no longer something I occasionally use. It’s part of my default workflow.

---

That said, it’s far from perfect.

There’s still friction. Getting good results depends on how well you ask, and even when something looks correct, you still need to verify it. I’ve been trying to reduce that friction (for example, by using voice instead of typing), but it’s still a work in progress.

---

I actually tried “vibe coding” last year.

It didn’t go well. Projects broke, things weren’t stable, and I lost progress more than once. It was frustrating enough that I just stopped.

But now I’m coming back to it with a different mindset.

I want to build something real: something that connects a frontend with a backend, and actually uses the concepts I’ve been learning along the way. And I want to do it fast.

---

This series is my way of documenting that process.

Not as a polished tutorial, but as a build log: what works, what doesn’t, and what surprises me along the way.

Partly for myself. Partly for anyone curious about what it actually looks like to build with AI right now.

And yes, also to show what I can build.
